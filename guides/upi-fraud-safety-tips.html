UPI Fraud: 12 Safety Rules Every Indian Smartphone User Should Know

How common UPI scams work — fake collect requests, screen-sharing, QR tricks, fake support numbers — and simple rules that keep your money safe.

By AppBharat Editorial · Updated 17 Nov 2026 · 7 min read ·

UPI has made payments instant and free for hundreds of millions of people. Unfortunately, scammers have adapted too. Almost every UPI fraud relies on one thing: tricking you into approving a payment. The system itself is secure; the weak point is the moment you enter your PIN.

The golden rule

You never need to enter your UPI PIN to receive money. If anyone asks you to scan a QR code, approve a request or enter your PIN "to receive" a payment, it is a scam.

How the most common scams work

  • Fake collect requests. A "buyer" on a resale site sends a collect request instead of paying. Approving it sends money out.
  • QR code tricks. You're asked to scan a QR "to get your refund". Scanning and entering a PIN pays the scammer.
  • Screen-sharing apps. Fake bank or customer-care agents ask you to install a screen-sharing or "support" app, then watch you type PINs and OTPs.
  • Fake customer-care numbers. Scammers post fake helpline numbers online and in search ads. Always use the number inside the official app or on the official website.
  • "Wrong transfer" pressure. Someone claims they sent money to you by mistake and pressures you to send it back — often after a fake SMS that looks like a credit.
  • Task and investment scams. "Like videos and earn" schemes pay small amounts first, then ask you to "invest" larger sums you never get back.

12 rules to stay safe

  1. Never share your UPI PIN, OTP or card CVV with anyone, including "bank staff".
  2. Check your bank balance in your bank app — don't trust an SMS alone.
  3. Read the screen before entering a PIN: does it say "Pay" or "Debit"?
  4. Decline collect requests from strangers.
  5. Never install remote-access or screen-sharing apps at someone's request.
  6. Find customer-care numbers only inside the official app or website.
  7. Set a daily UPI limit in your app if it offers one.
  8. Keep your phone, UPI apps and banking apps updated.
  9. Use a screen lock and app lock.
  10. Be suspicious of urgency, threats or deals that look too good.
  11. Verify the recipient name shown before you confirm.
  12. Report suspicious numbers and messages through the Sanchar Saathi platform.

If you've been defrauded

Act fast. Call the national cybercrime helpline 1930 or file a complaint on cybercrime.gov.in, and contact your bank immediately to request that the transaction be flagged. The sooner you report, the better the chance of freezing funds.
