App Permissions and Your Privacy: A Practical Guide for Indian Users

Which app permissions are risky, how to review them on Android and iPhone, and what India's data protection law means for you.

By AppBharat Editorial · Updated 17 Nov 2026 · 6 min read ·

Every app you install asks for permissions — access to your camera, location, contacts, files or SMS. Most requests are legitimate, but over-permissioned apps are a common route for data misuse and fraud. Five minutes of review can close those doors.

The high-risk permissions

  • SMS: can read OTPs. Very few apps need this.
  • Contacts: commonly misused by illegal lending apps to harass borrowers' friends and family.
  • Accessibility services: lets an app read your screen and tap for you — a favourite of banking malware. Grant it only to trusted apps that genuinely need it.
  • Display over other apps: can be abused to show fake screens.
  • Install unknown apps: lets an app install other apps.
  • Location "all the time": rarely necessary; "while using the app" is usually enough.

How to review permissions

Android: Settings → Security & privacy (or Privacy) → Permission manager. Pick a permission, such as SMS, and see which apps have it. Android can also automatically remove permissions from apps you haven't used for a while.

iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security. Review each category, and check the App Privacy Report to see what apps actually accessed.

A simple rule

Ask: does this permission make sense for what the app does? A torch app doesn't need your contacts. A calculator doesn't need your location.

Your rights under Indian law

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 sets rules for how organisations handle personal data in India. In broad terms, companies need a lawful basis such as your consent, must tell you what they collect and why, and must let you access, correct and erase your data and withdraw consent. The implementing rules were notified in 2025, with obligations phased in over time. If an app misuses your data, start with its grievance officer, whose contact details should be in the privacy policy.

Five-minute privacy routine

  1. Delete apps you haven't used in three months.
  2. Revoke SMS and contacts access where it isn't essential.
  3. Turn off ad personalisation in your phone's settings if you prefer.
  4. Keep your OS and apps updated.
  5. Install apps only from official stores.
